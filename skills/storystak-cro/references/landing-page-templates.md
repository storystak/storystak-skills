# Landing Page Templates — Awareness-Level Matched

> **Purpose:** Use these templates to audit single-purpose page structure or build new pages. Each template is matched to a traffic temperature and Schwartz Awareness Level. The section order matters — it follows the psychological sequence that moves a visitor from where they are to the CTA.
>
> **Single-purpose pages** have no site navigation, one CTA, and one job: convert. They live on landing-page hosts (Unbounce, Webflow LP, Typeform, Perspective, ClickFunnels), or in stripped-down corners of the main site. If the page has site nav, use `references/website-page-framework.md` instead.

## Template 1: Cold Traffic Landing Page (Problem Aware)

**Traffic source:** Paid social (Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn), paid search, display, pre-roll
**Awareness level:** Problem Aware — they know the problem exists, may not know a solution category exists
**Primary emotional lever:** Pulled from client context — usually the #1 lever for cold traffic
**Goal:** The primary CTA from client context (lead capture, free trial, demo, free kit, quote, etc.)

### Section Order

**1. Selectivity Bar (sticky or top banner) — Optional**
Use when the client offer is selective and the selectivity is real. Examples: "We accept 1 in 4 applicants." "Available to teams with 50+ engineers." "Only 12 client slots open this quarter."

Skip this section if selectivity isn't part of the offer or feels forced.

**2. Hero Section — Above the Fold**
- **Pain-point or outcome headline** matched to the ad's hook (message match is critical — if the ad says "X," the page must visibly start with "X")
- **1–2 sentence sub-headline** that agitates the specific pain or amplifies the outcome
- **Form (2 fields max — typically Name + Email)** OR **single CTA button** routing to a quiz/calendar/multi-step form
- **CTA button copy:** action + value ("Get My Free Audit," "Start My Trial," "See If I Qualify"). Never "Submit," "Send," "Contact Us."
- **CTA support text:** 2–4 stacked friction reducers ("Free. No credit card. Takes 30 seconds.")
- **Trust stat line below form:** 3 stats from client context's approved list

**3. Trust Bar (immediately below hero)**
- Key stats in a horizontal bar: customers/users/clients served, outcome metric (revenue saved, time saved, weight lost, etc.), reviews / rating, years in business
- Recognized credentials, badges, or logos (Inc. 5000, SOC 2, customer logos, press mentions)
- Keep this above the scroll line or immediately below it

**4. Pain Agitation Section**
- Name 2–3 specific pain points the audience experiences
- Use the dominant emotional lever from client context
- Each pain: short paragraph (2–3 sentences), ends with a punch line
- Common formats: "The 3 [Lies / Mistakes / Reasons] That [Outcome]" — the "rule of 3" structure outperforms longer or shorter lists in most tests

**5. Specificity / Math Block**
- Concrete numbers that make the pain or opportunity real
- "X today. At current rates, that's Y in 10 years."
- "The average [audience] loses $X / Y hours / Z opportunities to this every [time period]."
- Source attribution where applicable (industry reports, customer data, client research)

**6. Founder Origin Story (if applicable per client context)**
- 3–4 sentences max
- Personal experience → turning point → current mission
- "Industry insider turned advocate" frame works in nearly every vertical where it applies
- Photo of founder if available
- Subject to compliance constraints in the client context

**7. The Process / Named Mechanism**
- The Brunson "proprietary mechanism" — the named method, framework, or system the client uses
- 3-step visual: Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3
- Each step: 1 sentence, icon or number
- Emphasize what makes the process different from the alternatives

**8. Testimonials**
- 2–3 testimonials matched to the page's emotional lever and traffic temperature (per `references/review-selector.md`)
- Named, specific outcomes: "Sarah cut her invoice processing from 15 hours/week to 2"
- Star rating + total review count
- Embed video testimonial if available

**9. Risk Reversal / Guarantee (if applicable)**
- Money-back guarantees, free trials, "we don't get paid unless you do" — whatever risk reversal the client offer carries
- Subject to compliance: many regulated industries restrict guarantee language

**10. CTA Repeat**
- Same headline + form as hero, or simplified
- Option to reframe: "Still not sure? [Selectivity statement / Trust stat]. We're confident we can help."

**11. Footer Disclaimer**
- Required compliance disclaimer per client context
- No navigation links that could leak traffic (single-focus page)

## Template 2: Warm Traffic Landing Page (Solution Aware)

**Traffic source:** Email nurture, retargeting, organic search for solution terms
**Awareness level:** Solution Aware — they know the solution category exists, comparing options
**Primary emotional lever:** Selectivity + differentiation + authority proof
**Goal:** Conversion or qualification quiz

### Section Order

**1. Differentiation Headline**
- Lead with what makes this client different, not the pain
- "The only [category] that [unique mechanism]"
- "Built for [specific audience], not for [generic audience]"

**2. Hero Sub-Headline**
- Sharper specificity than cold-traffic copy
- Name the alternative they're comparing against (without naming competitors if compliance restricts it)
- "If you've tried [alternative approach] and it didn't work, here's why."

**3. Authority Proof Stack**
- Customer logos / named clients (if approved per client context)
- Press mentions, awards, certifications
- Founder credentials if relevant
- Specific case study teaser with named outcome

**4. The Differentiator Explained**
- 2–3 specific things this client does differently
- Each backed by proof, not just claim
- Example: "We use [proprietary method] — competitors use [generic alternative]. Here's why that matters: [specific outcome]."

**5. Comparison or "Why Us" Section**
- Side-by-side comparison vs. status quo or alternative approach
- If competitor naming is allowed per client context: vs. specific competitors
- If not allowed: "vs. legacy approach" or "vs. doing it yourself"

**6. Detailed Process Walkthrough**
- More depth than cold-traffic process section
- Warm visitors want to understand the "how" before committing
- Address the 2–3 most common questions about the process

**7. Featured Case Study or Testimonials**
- 2–3 longer-format testimonials (medium narrative)
- Named clients with specific, quantified outcomes
- Prefer testimonials that handle skepticism: "I'd tried 3 other [solutions] before this one, and..."

**8. CTA**
- Higher-commitment than cold-traffic CTA: "Book a strategy call," "Apply to work with us," "Take the qualification quiz"
- The visitor is more invested — match the CTA to that investment

**9. Footer Disclaimer**

## Template 3: Advertorial / Long-Form Sales Page

**Traffic source:** Paid social, native ads, content distribution networks
**Awareness level:** Problem Aware → Solution Aware (the page does the awareness journey)
**Primary emotional lever:** Whatever the lead emotional lever is, deeply explored
**Goal:** Single CTA at the end (often after 1,500–4,000 words)

### Section Order

**1. Headline + Editorial Framing**
- Reads like an article, not an ad
- Editorial-style headline: "How [Specific Person] [Specific Outcome] (And Why It's Possible for [Audience Like You])"
- Byline or "as featured in" framing where appropriate
- Compliance: editorial-disguised ads have FTC disclosure rules — check client context for required disclaimers

**2. Story Hook Opening**
- Start with a person, an event, or a moment — not a sales pitch
- "Last Tuesday, Sarah did something that surprised her CFO..."
- Ride the story for 2–4 paragraphs before any product mention

**3. Problem Exploration**
- Take 4–6 paragraphs to deeply explore the problem the audience faces
- Statistics, quotes, expert references
- Build the conviction that this problem is bigger and more expensive than the reader realized

**4. False Solutions / DIY Failures**
- "Most people try [X], [Y], or [Z] — here's why those don't work"
- Validates the reader's prior failed attempts
- Sets up the client's solution as the missing piece

**5. The Reveal — Introduce the Solution**
- This is where the product/service finally enters
- Frame as discovery, not pitch: "Then I found out about [client name]..."
- Brief mechanism explanation — how it works differently

**6. The Mechanism in Detail**
- Explain the named mechanism (Brunson proprietary mechanism)
- Why it works, what makes it different
- Specific outcomes, named customer examples

**7. Proof Stack**
- Multiple testimonials embedded throughout the next sections
- Statistics, case studies, named clients
- Mix of formats: short pull quotes + longer narratives

**8. Objection Handling**
- Anticipate the 3–5 most common objections (cost, time, complexity, "is this for me?", "will this work for me specifically?")
- Address each in a paragraph
- Use real customer quotes where possible: "I had the same concern, but..."

**9. Risk Reversal**
- Guarantee, free trial, money-back, or whatever the offer carries
- Subject to compliance

**10. The Offer**
- Clear, specific offer with all components listed
- Price (if direct-sale advertorial) or CTA value framing (if lead gen)

**11. CTA Block**
- After all the buildup, this should feel like a relief, not a pitch
- "Click below to [action]"
- Friction reducers stacked

**12. Footer + Required Disclaimers**

## Template 4: Thank-You Page

**Traffic source:** Internal (post-conversion)
**Goal:** Set expectations, reduce buyer's remorse, drive secondary action

### Section Order

**1. Confirmation Banner**
- "Your [thing] is on its way."
- Visual confirmation (checkmark, celebration graphic — restrained)
- Set the next-step expectation immediately

**2. What Happens Next**
- 3-step visual or numbered list of what they'll experience
- Set time expectations: "You'll get the kit within 5–7 business days," "Your specialist will call within 24 hours"
- Reduce uncertainty

**3. Trust Reinforcement**
- 1 testimonial or stat that reinforces they made the right choice
- Reduces buyer's remorse / cancellation likelihood
- Per `references/review-selector.md` — choose a "skepticism overcome" or "vindication" testimonial

**4. Bridge / Secondary Action**
- Calendar embed if they need to book a call
- Digital downloadable bridge content while physical fulfillment ships
- "Watch this 5-minute video while you wait"
- Add to calendar / set reminder

**5. Optional Information Capture**
- Now that they've converted, ask for the field you didn't ask upfront
- "One more thing — to help us prepare your [thing], can you tell us [phone / company / use case]?"
- Foot-in-the-door: easier to ask after a yes

**6. Social Sharing (where appropriate)**
- "Know someone who might need this?" share buttons
- Referral program entry if the client has one

## Template 5: Quiz Funnel

**Traffic source:** Paid social, organic content
**Awareness level:** Variable — quiz format works across awareness levels
**Primary emotional lever:** Curiosity + personalization
**Goal:** Capture lead through progressive commitment

### Section Order

**1. Quiz Intro Page**
- Compelling quiz title: "Find Out If You Qualify in 60 Seconds" or "Discover Your [Type / Score / Match]"
- 1-sentence promise of what they'll learn
- "Start the Quiz" CTA
- Time / question count expectation: "8 questions, 60 seconds"

**2. Question Screens (5–8 questions max)**
- Easy questions first: button taps, not typing
- Each question advances a progress bar
- Mix of: identifying questions ("Do you currently...?"), severity questions ("How much does X cost you per month?"), context questions ("Have you tried [alternative] before?")
- The questions themselves are content — they validate the visitor's experience

**3. Loading / Calculating Screen**
- 3–5 second "calculating your results" animation
- Sets up the reveal
- Optional: "Analyzing 50,000 data points to find your match..."

**4. Email Capture (just before results)**
- "Where should we send your results?"
- Single-field email + name (or just email)
- This is the primary conversion event — the rest of the quiz served to earn it

**5. Results Page**
- Personalized result based on their answers
- Specific recommendation tied to the client's offer
- CTA to book / buy / request kit

### Why Quiz Funnels Work
- **Cialdini Commitment & Consistency:** small "yes" answers build toward the conversion
- **Cialdini Reciprocity:** they receive personalized value (the result) before being asked to commit
- **Hormozi Effort & Sacrifice:** typing is high effort; button taps are low effort
- **Trust building:** each question that "feels right" builds confidence in the brand

## Universal Single-Purpose Page Rules

### Strip Site Navigation
The defining feature of a single-purpose page is no escape routes. Strip the header nav and footer links. The only paths off the page should be the CTA or the back button.

### One CTA, Repeated
Every CTA on the page should drive to the same conversion event. Don't mix "Request a demo" with "Download the ebook" with "Subscribe to the newsletter." Pick one.

### Mobile-First Design
60–80% of traffic to single-purpose pages is mobile. Design mobile first, ensure form is reachable without excessive scrolling, ensure CTA tap targets are 44px+, ensure no overlapping widgets (chat, popups) cover the CTA.

### Message Match the Source
The headline of the landing page must visibly match the message the visitor clicked to get here. If the ad said "Cut invoice processing time by 80%," the page should open with that exact promise. Mismatched messaging is the single biggest reason pages don't convert.

### Speed
Pages over 3 seconds to first contentful paint lose visitors before they can convert. Optimize images, defer scripts, host on a fast CDN.

### Track What Matters
Per the client's analytics setup (or `analytics-tracking` skill): track form starts, field-level drop-off, completion rate, scroll depth, time on page, and source attribution. Without these, you can't iterate.
