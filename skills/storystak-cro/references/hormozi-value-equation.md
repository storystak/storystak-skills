# Hormozi Value Equation — A Diagnostic for Offer Strength

> **Purpose:** Alex Hormozi's Value Equation isn't just a theory — it's a diagnostic. When a page isn't converting, you can almost always trace the problem to one of the four variables. This reference shows how to score a page against the equation and how to fix each variable.

---

## The Equation

```
              Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
  Value  =  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                   Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```

The two variables on top (Dream Outcome, Perceived Likelihood) you want to **maximize**.
The two variables on the bottom (Time Delay, Effort & Sacrifice) you want to **minimize**.

A page that scores high on all four is a "Grand Slam" offer in Hormozi's framing. Most pages fail one or two — that's where the conversion leak lives.

---

## How to Use This as an Audit Tool

For any page you audit, score each variable 1–10. The lowest-scoring variable is your conversion bottleneck.

| Variable | What You're Asking | 1 (Bad) | 10 (Great) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dream Outcome** | How vividly is the desired result articulated? | "We help you grow" (vague, no destination) | "From 11 hours/week chasing approvals to 11 minutes" (specific, vivid, named numbers) |
| **Perceived Likelihood** | How believable is it that this offer delivers the outcome? | No proof, no track record, no specifics | Specific case studies, named clients, transparent process, third-party validation |
| **Time Delay** | How fast does the visitor get the outcome? | "Results take 6–12 months" with no quick wins | "First win within 7 days, full ROI within 60" |
| **Effort & Sacrifice** | How much work / risk / discomfort is required? | Long onboarding, manual setup, behavior change required | "We handle setup. 5-minute onboarding. Cancel anytime." |

Total Value = (Dream × Likelihood) ÷ (Delay × Effort)

Even if you score 9/10 on Dream Outcome and Likelihood, scoring 2/10 on Time Delay and 2/10 on Effort means:
**(9 × 9) ÷ (2 × 2) = wait, that's actually 81 ÷ 4 = 20** — high value
But: **(9 × 9) ÷ (8 × 8) = 81 ÷ 64 = 1.27** — low value

The denominator matters more than people realize. Pages with strong outcomes but high friction or long delay underperform pages with modest outcomes and low friction / fast delivery.

## Variable 1: Dream Outcome

### What to Audit

Read the page and ask: "If I'm the visitor, what specifically do I get?"

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **1–3** | Vague. "Grow your business." "Improve your health." "Save time." No specifics, no destination. |
| **4–6** | Some specificity, but generic. "Save up to 10 hours a week." "Lose weight in 30 days." Numbers without context. |
| **7–8** | Specific and vivid. "Stop spending 11 hours a week chasing AP approvals." Named pain, quantified outcome. |
| **9–10** | Specific, vivid, AND aspirational. Outcome connects to identity or status, not just function. "Be the CFO whose AP team works on strategy, not chase emails." |

### How to Improve It

- **Add specific numbers** — "Save time" → "Save 11 hours/week"
- **Name the alternative they're escaping** — "No more sleepless nights" → "No more 2 AM panic about whether you'll make payroll"
- **Connect to identity** — "Grow your business" → "Build the business you can actually sell in 5 years"
- **Use customer-language outcomes** — Pull verbatim language from testimonials, sales calls, customer interviews
- **Make it visual** — Where possible, show the outcome (before/after, dashboard screenshot, testimonial video)

### Common Failure Modes

- **Feature listing instead of outcome stating:** "Includes invoicing, expense tracking, and reporting" → describes what's in the box, not what changes for the buyer
- **Industry jargon:** "Drive operational efficiency through automation" → no human visualizes this
- **The "more / better / faster" trap:** "More leads, better quality, faster pipeline" — relative claims with no anchor
- **Aspirational without specificity:** "Live the life you've always wanted" — without a vivid picture of what that life looks like

## Variable 2: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement

### What to Audit

Read the page and ask: "Does the visitor believe this client can actually deliver?"

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **1–3** | No proof, no track record, no specifics. "Trust us." |
| **4–6** | Generic trust signals. "Thousands of customers." "Years of experience." "5-star reviews." No specifics. |
| **7–8** | Specific proof. Named customers, quantified outcomes, established track record. "Used by Stripe, Notion, and 500+ finance teams." |
| **9–10** | Specific proof + risk reversal + transparent process. The visitor can see exactly how the outcome happens AND has a safety net if it doesn't. |

### How to Improve It

- **Stack specific stats** from client context (customers served, outcome metrics, longevity)
- **Show named customers** (logos, case studies, named testimonials)
- **Detail the process** — show exactly how the outcome happens, not just claim it
- **Add risk reversal** (compliance-permitting): money-back, free trial, "we don't get paid unless you do"
- **Third-party validation** — press mentions, awards, certifications, aggregate review widgets
- **Founder credibility** — if the founder's story builds belief, deploy it

### Common Failure Modes

- **Generic trust badges** — "5-star reviews" without showing the reviews; "trusted by thousands" without naming any
- **Vague guarantees** — "Money-back guarantee" without saying what triggers it
- **Polished case studies that feel manufactured** — when prospect senses the case study is too perfect, belief drops
- **Founder credibility that doesn't connect** — "Founded by a Stanford MBA" doesn't help if the buyer doesn't care about Stanford MBAs

## Variable 3: Time Delay

### What to Audit

Read the page and ask: "How fast does the visitor get the result?"

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **1–3** | "Results take 6–12 months" / "It's a long process" / no timeline given (which the visitor assumes is bad) |
| **4–6** | Reasonable timeline stated, but no quick wins. "Onboarding takes 30 days." "First report in 60 days." |
| **7–8** | Timeline stated AND quick wins promised. "Onboarded in 7 days. First win in 30. Full ROI by day 90." |
| **9–10** | Instant or near-instant value at the start. "Live in 5 minutes. First insight in 15." |

### How to Improve It

- **State the timeline explicitly** — visitors fear what they don't know
- **Promise quick wins** — even if the full outcome takes time, what does the visitor get in week 1?
- **Provide bridge content** — for offers with delivery delays, give them something immediately while they wait
- **Speed up onboarding visibly** — "5-minute setup" beats "fast setup"
- **Compress the delivery process** where possible — "next-day shipping," "overnight setup," "same-day appointment"

### Common Failure Modes

- **No timeline stated** — visitors assume the worst
- **Long onboarding without quick wins** — even a 90-day onboarding feels manageable if there's a 7-day win
- **"Custom timeline" — depends on you" framing** — feels like an excuse, transfers responsibility to the visitor
- **Burying the timeline in FAQ** — should be on the main page, not hidden

### When Time Delay Is Genuinely Long

Some offers — high-touch services, regulated industries, complex implementations — have unavoidably long timelines. In those cases:

- **Acknowledge it openly** — pretending it's fast when it isn't damages trust
- **Compress what you can** — fast onboarding kickoff, fast first conversation, fast bridge deliverable
- **Reframe the long delivery as part of the value** — "It takes 4–6 months because we don't cut corners" (only if true)
- **Stack quick wins along the timeline** — "Day 1: kickoff. Week 1: discovery. Week 2: first deliverable. Month 3: full implementation."

## Variable 4: Effort & Sacrifice

### What to Audit

Read the page and ask: "How much work, risk, or discomfort is required from the visitor?"

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **1–3** | High effort signaled. Long forms, complex setup, behavior change required, manual work, learning curve. |
| **4–6** | Standard effort. Some forms, some setup, modest commitment. |
| **7–8** | Low effort. Short forms, automated setup, minimal behavior change. "We handle the heavy lifting." |
| **9–10** | Done-for-you. "Send us [X], we do the rest." OR "5-minute setup, no engineer needed." |

### How to Improve It

- **Reduce form fields** to the minimum (see `references/form-optimization.md`)
- **Stack friction reducers** at the CTA — "Free. No credit card. 30 seconds."
- **Position the offer as done-for-you** where applicable
- **Remove decision burden** — fewer choices, clearer recommendations, smart defaults
- **Address commitment fears** — "Cancel anytime," "No long-term contract," "Month-to-month"
- **Show what THEY have to do (which should be minimal)** vs. what the client handles

### Common Failure Modes

- **Long forms** — every additional field is effort
- **Vague onboarding** — "We'll work with you to set it up" sounds high-effort
- **No explicit "what we handle" section** — visitor assumes they have to do everything
- **Hidden commitments** — annual-only billing surfaced late, automatic renewals, hidden cancellation friction
- **Behavior-change-required offers without acknowledgment** — diet products, fitness apps, productivity habits — these need to address the effort honestly and reduce it where possible

## Bonus Levers (Hormozi Value Stacks)

Hormozi recommends stacking additional perceived value through bonuses, scarcity, and urgency. Used carefully — and within compliance:

### Bonus Stacks
- "Sign up today and get [Bonus 1: $X value], [Bonus 2: $Y value], [Bonus 3: $Z value] — total bonus value: $X+Y+Z"
- Each bonus should solve a related problem the visitor has
- Use only when the bonuses are real and deliverable

### Scarcity / Urgency
- Real cart deadlines ("Doors close Friday at midnight")
- Real cohort limits ("12 spots per quarter")
- Real inventory ("Only 47 units left")
- **NEVER fake scarcity** — visitors detect it and conversion drops

### Risk Reversal
- Money-back guarantees (compliance-permitting)
- Free trials with no credit card
- "We don't get paid unless you do" (high-ticket service framing)
- "Try it for 30 days" with full refund

These don't change the fundamentals — Dream × Likelihood ÷ Delay ÷ Effort still rules — but they amplify perceived value.

## Putting It All Together: The Audit Question

For any page, ask:

> "Does this page maximize the **Dream Outcome** and **Perceived Likelihood**, while minimizing **Time Delay** and **Effort & Sacrifice**?"

Score each 1–10. The lowest score is the conversion bottleneck. Fix that first.

Common patterns:

| Bottleneck | What's Usually Happening | What to Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low Dream Outcome | Vague "we help you grow" copy | Add specific numbers, named outcomes, identity connection |
| Low Likelihood | No proof, generic trust | Stack specific stats, named customers, risk reversal |
| High Time Delay | No timeline, slow onboarding | State timeline, add quick wins, compress where possible |
| High Effort | Long forms, complex setup | Reduce fields, position as done-for-you, stack friction reducers |

A page with all four scoring 7+ converts well. A page with three 7s and one 3 leaks at the 3.

## Worked Example

**Page:** B2B SaaS demo request page for AP automation tool

**Dream Outcome (current):** "Streamline your accounts payable process with our AI-powered platform" → **Score: 4** (vague, no specific outcome)

**Perceived Likelihood (current):** Logo bar with 8 customers, "trusted by 500+ teams" → **Score: 6** (some proof, but no named case studies, no specific outcomes per customer)

**Time Delay (current):** "Get a personalized demo" with no timeline → **Score: 4** (visitor doesn't know how long the demo takes, how long until value)

**Effort & Sacrifice (current):** 7-field demo request form, no friction reducers → **Score: 3** (high friction)

**Diagnosis:** The bottleneck is **Effort & Sacrifice** (3/10). Even though Dream Outcome and Likelihood need work, the form friction is killing conversions before the rest gets a chance to matter.

**Fix priority:**
1. Reduce form to 4 fields (work email, company, role, company size). Add friction reducers.
2. Sharpen Dream Outcome: "Cut invoice processing from 11 hours/week to 11 minutes."
3. Add 1–2 named customer case studies with specific outcomes.
4. State demo timeline: "30-minute demo, custom to your AP workflow. Live access within 24 hours of sign-up."

After: Dream Outcome 7, Likelihood 8, Delay 7, Effort 7. Total value rises significantly.
