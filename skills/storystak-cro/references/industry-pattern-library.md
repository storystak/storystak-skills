# Industry Pattern Library — CRO by Vertical

> **Purpose:** Day-one intelligence for clients in common verticals. Use this as input alongside the client context, not as a replacement for it. Each vertical has typical awareness levels, dominant emotional levers, common CTA patterns, and common compliance traps. The patterns are defaults — the client's actual data overrides them.

---

## How to Use This File

1. Identify the client's vertical from the client context.
2. Skim the vertical's section below.
3. Apply the patterns as **starting hypotheses** — confirm against the client's actual audience research, ad performance, or test data.
4. Flag any vertical-typical compliance trap that the client context doesn't already address.

If the client doesn't fit cleanly into any vertical here (or sits at an intersection), pull patterns from the closest 2 and note where they conflict.

## B2B SaaS

### Typical Audience
- Buyers: PMs, engineering leads, ops leads, department heads, VPs
- Stakeholders: technical evaluators + economic buyers + sometimes procurement
- Reading level: 9th–11th grade for marketing pages, 11th–college for docs
- Tech comfort: high
- Decision cycle: 2 weeks (SMB) to 6 months (enterprise)

### Awareness Levels by Channel
| Channel | Typical Awareness |
|---|---|
| Branded search | Most Aware |
| Category search ("invoice automation software") | Solution Aware |
| Comparison search ("Stripe vs. Braintree") | Product Aware |
| Cold paid social (LinkedIn, Twitter) | Problem Aware |
| SEO blog content | Unaware → Problem Aware |
| Outbound email | Often Unaware (interrupting) |

### Dominant Emotional Levers
1. **Time waste / manual work** — "Stop spending 10 hours a week on X"
2. **Risk reduction** — "Don't fail SOC 2," "Don't ship broken code"
3. **Status / career** — "Be the PM who shipped this"
4. **Team morale** — "Your team will thank you"
5. **Cost transparency** — "Cut spend by X% without firing anyone"

### Common CTA Patterns
- Self-serve: "Start free trial," "Try free," "Sign up free" (PLG)
- Sales-led: "Get a demo," "Talk to sales," "See it in action"
- Hybrid: "Try free or talk to sales"
- Bridge: "Calculate your ROI," "See your audit results"

### Conversion Heuristics
- **Free trial** > "Request demo" for SMB tools under $500/mo
- **Demo request** > free trial for enterprise tools where setup is non-trivial
- **No-credit-card friction reducer** is essential for trial CTAs
- **G2 / Capterra badges** outperform generic trust logos for vetted-buyer audiences
- **Customer logo bar** (recognizable brands) is the single most-tested trust signal — works almost universally

### Common Compliance Traps
- "SOC 2 compliant" vs. "SOC 2 certified" — these are different; misuse can trigger legal issues
- Performance claims need substantiation (actual customer data, not modeled estimates)
- Competitor comparison pages: legal teams often restrict naming or require disclaimers
- "Enterprise-grade" is vague but generally safe; "bank-grade security" can trigger regulatory scrutiny

## DTC E-Commerce (Consumer Products)

### Typical Audience
- Buyers: end consumers, often female-skewed depending on category
- Reading level: 6th–8th grade
- Mobile-first: typically 60–80% of traffic
- Decision cycle: minutes to hours (impulse) or days (considered purchases)

### Awareness Levels by Channel
| Channel | Typical Awareness |
|---|---|
| Branded search / direct | Most Aware |
| Category search ("blue light glasses") | Solution Aware |
| Cold paid social (Meta, TikTok) | Problem Aware |
| Influencer content | Often Unaware → Problem Aware |
| Email to existing customers | Most Aware |

### Dominant Emotional Levers
1. **Solve a felt pain** — visible problem the buyer experiences daily
2. **Identity / status** — what owning this product says about you
3. **Belonging / community** — "join 100k+ people who..."
4. **FOMO / scarcity** — limited drops, low stock, time-bound offers
5. **Price anchoring** — "$99 value, today $39"

### Common CTA Patterns
- "Shop now," "Buy now," "Get yours"
- Quiz funnels for personalization ("Find your shade")
- Subscription with first-bottle-free or 50%-off-first-month
- Bundle pricing with anchored discount

### Conversion Heuristics
- **Mobile-first design is non-negotiable** — desktop is a secondary experience
- **Reviews + UGC outperform polished product photography** for credibility
- **Hero video showing the product in use** outperforms static hero images
- **Sticky add-to-cart bar** on mobile is a default
- **"Free shipping over $X"** is a strong upsell driver
- **Apple Pay / Shop Pay / Klarna** at checkout reduce friction significantly

### Common Compliance Traps
- Health/wellness claims (FDA, FTC) — "supports" / "may help" not "cures" / "treats"
- "Clinically proven" requires actual clinical studies
- Influencer/endorser disclosures (FTC #ad rules)
- Auto-ship / subscription dark patterns (state attorney general scrutiny)
- "Made in USA" claims (FTC has strict rules on what qualifies)
- Before/after photos in beauty/wellness (Meta and Google both restrict)

## High-Ticket Services (Agencies, Consultants, Coaches, Done-For-You)

### Typical Audience
- Buyers: founders, executives, owners of small/mid businesses, individual professionals
- Reading level: 8th–10th grade for most; higher for ultra-high-ticket
- Decision cycle: weeks to months
- Trust threshold: very high — they're handing over budget and outcomes

### Awareness Levels by Channel
| Channel | Typical Awareness |
|---|---|
| Referral / word of mouth | Most Aware |
| Cold outbound | Unaware |
| Cold paid (Meta, LinkedIn) | Problem Aware |
| Webinar / workshop funnels | Solution Aware → Product Aware |
| Branded content / podcast | Variable |

### Dominant Emotional Levers
1. **Time / opportunity cost** — "Stop trying to figure this out yourself"
2. **Done-for-you ease** — "We handle it end to end"
3. **Specific outcome** — "From X to Y in N weeks"
4. **Selectivity** — "We accept N clients per quarter"
5. **Authority** — credentials, named clients, case study specificity

### Common CTA Patterns
- "Book a discovery call," "Schedule a strategy session"
- "Apply to work with us" (selectivity frame)
- "Get a custom proposal"
- Bridge: "Take the assessment," "Get your audit," "Download the framework"

### Conversion Heuristics
- **Selectivity bars work especially well** — "We work with 12 clients per quarter, currently 3 spots open"
- **Founder-led pages outperform corporate-voice pages** for sub-$50k engagements
- **Specific case study with named client** > generic "trusted by" logos
- **Calendar embeds** outperform "request a call" forms (commitment + frictionless booking)
- **Long-form pages outperform short** — high-ticket buyers want detail before booking
- **Video testimonials > written** for trust at this price point

### Common Compliance Traps
- Income / results claims (FTC) — coaching especially scrutinized
- "Guaranteed" results — almost always a legal risk
- Testimonial cherry-picking without "results not typical" disclaimers
- State professional licensing rules (financial advice, legal advice, therapy adjacent)

## Lead-Gen for Regulated High-Ticket Services (Legal, Financial, Medical-Adjacent)

### Typical Audience
- Buyers: consumers in distress, often older
- Reading level: 6th–8th grade
- Trust threshold: very high, often after being burned
- Mobile-first: yes, but desktop matters too for older skew

### Awareness Levels by Channel
| Channel | Typical Awareness |
|---|---|
| Cold paid social | Unaware → Problem Aware |
| Search intent ("how to get out of X") | Problem Aware → Solution Aware |
| Referral | Solution Aware → Most Aware |
| Re-engagement | Solution Aware (already in funnel) |

### Dominant Emotional Levers
1. **Deception / "you were lied to"** — externalized villain
2. **Financial pain (specific math)** — concrete numbers beat vague claims
3. **Trapped / no way out**
4. **Inheritance / family burden** (older audiences)
5. **Selectivity** — reframes from "buy us" to "see if you qualify"
6. **Founder origin story** (industry-insider-turned-advocate is gold)

### Common CTA Patterns
- "Get your free [Kit / Guide / Audit]"
- "See if you qualify"
- "Request your free consultation"
- Avoid: "Schedule a call" (high friction for cold traffic)

### Conversion Heuristics
- **Free physical mailed kits often outperform digital downloads** for older audiences
- **2-field hero forms** (name + email) beat 4+ fields almost universally
- **Founder origin story** is often the highest-leverage trust element
- **Selectivity** ("we decline 40%") doubles as trust + scarcity signal
- **Older-audience UX rules**: 16px+ font, 44px+ tap targets, single-column mobile, visible labels (not placeholder-only)

### Common Compliance Traps (Highly Variable by Industry)
- **Legal-adjacent:** "lawyer," "attorney," "lawsuit," "legal," "contract" often restricted
- **Financial:** "guaranteed return," income claims, "FDIC-insured" misuse
- **Medical / health:** "cure," "treat," "diagnose," before/after claims
- **Debt / credit:** FTC TSR rules on debt relief, "stop paying" almost always restricted
- **Universal:** "money-back guarantee," "100%," "BBB" name use, "AARP" name use
- **State-specific:** consumer protection rules vary by state

This is the regulated-lead-gen vertical. If a client sells here, the compliance section of their context is the highest-stakes part of the entire setup.

## Info Products (Courses, Memberships, Communities)

### Typical Audience
- Buyers: consumers and prosumers seeking transformation, skill, or status
- Reading level: 7th–9th grade
- Decision cycle: minutes to weeks
- Trust threshold: variable — high during a launch, lower for evergreen

### Awareness Levels by Channel
| Channel | Typical Awareness |
|---|---|
| Email list (existing) | Solution Aware → Product Aware |
| Webinar funnel | Solution Aware → Product Aware |
| Cold paid social | Unaware → Problem Aware |
| Affiliate / influencer | Variable |
| Organic content (YouTube, podcast) | Variable, builds Most Aware over time |

### Dominant Emotional Levers
1. **Transformation / identity shift** — "Become the person who..."
2. **Speed / shortcut** — "Skip the years of trial and error"
3. **Belonging / community** — "Join 5,000 students who..."
4. **Authority / mentor** — "Learn from someone who's done it"
5. **Status / credentials** — "Get certified in..."

### Common CTA Patterns
- "Enroll now," "Join now," "Get instant access"
- Cart-open windows: "Doors close Friday at midnight"
- Bridge: "Watch the free training," "Take the quiz"
- Tripwire: low-cost entry product → main offer upsell

### Conversion Heuristics
- **Webinar / VSL funnels remain the dominant high-ticket conversion mechanism**
- **Cart-open windows with hard deadlines** outperform evergreen for big launches
- **Bonuses stacked at the CTA** drive perceived value (Hormozi Value Equation: stack the Dream Outcome)
- **Money-back guarantees** still convert here (where allowed)
- **Curriculum modules listed in detail** outperforms summary pitches

### Common Compliance Traps
- Income claims (FTC) — "students earn $X" requires substantiation
- Health/transformation claims for fitness/wellness courses
- "Limited spots" claims that aren't actually limited (deceptive scarcity)
- Affiliate disclosures
- Refund / guarantee terms must match what's delivered

## Local Service Businesses (Home Services, Med Spas, Local Pros)

### Typical Audience
- Buyers: homeowners, local consumers
- Reading level: 7th–9th grade
- Mobile-first: yes, often "I need this now" intent
- Decision cycle: hours to days

### Awareness Levels by Channel
| Channel | Typical Awareness |
|---|---|
| "Near me" search | Solution Aware → Most Aware |
| Google Maps / GMB | Solution Aware |
| Referral | Most Aware |
| Cold paid social | Problem Aware |

### Dominant Emotional Levers
1. **Urgency / immediate need** — "We can be there today"
2. **Trust / vetting** — "Licensed, insured, background-checked"
3. **Local proof** — "Serving [neighborhood] since [year]"
4. **Specific outcome** — "Roof replaced in 2 days"
5. **Avoiding bad outcomes** — "Don't get scammed"

### Common CTA Patterns
- "Get a free quote," "Schedule service," "Call now"
- Click-to-call CTAs prominent on mobile
- Online booking widgets where available
- "Same-day appointment" badges

### Conversion Heuristics
- **Click-to-call mobile CTA** is often the #1 conversion driver
- **Google Reviews aggregation widget** beats curated testimonials for local trust
- **Service area map** signals legitimacy
- **Before/after photos** convert in visible-results categories
- **License numbers + insurance details visible** reduces hesitation
- **Same-day / next-day availability** badges drive urgency conversions

### Common Compliance Traps
- State licensing display requirements (contractors, med spas, etc.)
- Before/after photo regulations (med spas especially — FDA territory)
- "Best in [city]" superlative claims
- Review solicitation rules (Google's policies)

## Marketplaces / Two-Sided Platforms

### Two Audiences = Two Sets of Rules
Marketplaces have to convert both supply and demand sides. Most CRO frameworks built for single-audience businesses break here.

- **Demand side (buyers):** typically follows the relevant DTC, B2B, or services pattern above
- **Supply side (sellers/providers):** follows the lead-gen pattern, with emphasis on earning potential, ease, and selectivity

### Common Compliance Traps
- Income / earnings claims for the supply side (FTC Business Opportunity Rule)
- "Guaranteed bookings" / "guaranteed income"
- Misrepresenting marketplace size to either side

## Cross-Vertical Patterns

A few patterns hold across nearly every vertical:

- **Specificity beats generality** — universal
- **Mobile-first design** — universal except for some enterprise B2B
- **Trust before form** — universal (more critical for cold/skeptical audiences)
- **Friction reducers stacked at CTA** — universal
- **Real testimonials > polished case studies** for credibility — universal
- **Founder/origin story builds trust fast** — works in nearly every B2C and most B2B contexts
- **Match copy to awareness level** — universal (and most-violated principle)

## When the Vertical Isn't Listed

If the client is in a vertical not covered here, do this:

1. Identify the closest analog (e.g., "B2B financial software" = closest to B2B SaaS, with regulated-services compliance)
2. Identify the audience analog (e.g., "doctors" might combine high-trust regulated-services with B2B-buyer behavior)
3. Pull patterns from the 2 closest verticals and note where they conflict
4. Flag the conflicts to the user — this is where their input matters most

Don't force-fit a client into the wrong vertical. If patterns conflict in a way that affects the audit, raise it.
