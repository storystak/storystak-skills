# Emotional Levers — The Generic Taxonomy

> **Purpose:** Use this file to choose the right emotional angle for any page, ad, or content piece — when the client context doesn't already specify a ranked set of validated levers. The taxonomy is universal; the validation is per-client.
>
> **Hard rule:** This file lists *candidate* levers and how to identify them. Which lever actually works best for a given client requires evidence — survey data, ad performance, customer interviews, or testing. Without evidence, your lever choice is a hypothesis. Flag it as such.

---

## How to Choose a Lever

If the client context lists ranked levers with validation evidence (survey data, ad performance, etc.), use those. Match the lever to the traffic temperature and awareness level per the client context.

If the client context has no ranked levers (or you're working from a URL-derived provisional context), follow this process:

1. **Read the client's current top-performing copy** — homepage hero, top-converting landing page, top-engaged ads. Which lever are they leaning on?
2. **Read 5–10 customer testimonials** — what emotions do customers describe when they talk about their problem and the resolution?
3. **Cross-reference the vertical** — `references/industry-pattern-library.md` lists typical levers per vertical
4. **Pick the 2–3 most likely levers** for the audience
5. **Flag them as untested hypotheses** unless you have validation evidence

Then use this guide to deploy the lever effectively.

## The Lever Taxonomy

Levers are organized by emotional category. Most pages use one primary lever and layer 1–2 secondary levers underneath.

---

### Category 1: External-Villain Levers

These work by externalizing the source of the problem — pointing at something the visitor was wronged by, lied to about, or trapped within.

#### Lever A: Deception / "You Were Lied To"
- **The frame:** "They told you X. The truth is Y."
- **Why it works:** Externalizes blame, validates the visitor's frustration, gives them permission to feel angry instead of foolish
- **When to use:** Industries with predatory sales practices, regulated industries with consumer-protection histories, audiences who feel they were misled at the point of sale
- **Watch-out:** The villain must be the practice or the system, NEVER a named competitor (unless client compliance explicitly approves competitor naming)
- **Example pattern:** "The 3 [Lies / Things They Don't Tell You / Mistakes They Sold You]"
- **Vertical fit:** Lead-gen for regulated services, anti-establishment positioning, alternative-to-incumbent pitches

#### Lever B: Hidden Cost / "Math They Don't Show You"
- **The frame:** "It looks like X, but here's the real number."
- **Why it works:** Concrete math beats vague pain. Visitors don't realize the true cost until you compute it for them.
- **When to use:** Anywhere the visitor underestimates the long-term cost of the status quo
- **Pattern:** "$X today. At [rate], that's $Y in 10 years."
- **Vertical fit:** Subscriptions, long-term financial obligations, recurring expenses, opportunity cost arguments

#### Lever C: Trapped / "No Way Out"
- **The frame:** "You can't sell it, can't return it, can't get out."
- **Why it works:** Validates accumulated resentment from long-term obligation
- **When to use:** Long-term contracts, perpetual obligations, industries with weak resale or exit markets
- **Vertical fit:** Timeshare exit, debt relief, contract cancellation, long-tenure SaaS lock-in

#### Lever D: System Working Against You
- **The frame:** "The fees you pay fund the lobby that fights against you."
- **Why it works:** Reveals an asymmetry the visitor didn't see; works as a "second-act" reveal
- **When to use:** Industries with regulatory capture, lobbying, or customer-hostile structures
- **Vertical fit:** Financial services reform, healthcare advocacy, consumer protection

---

### Category 2: Internal-Pain Levers

These work by naming a felt experience the visitor has but hasn't articulated.

#### Lever E: Time Waste
- **The frame:** "Stop spending [N hours] on [thing]."
- **Why it works:** Time loss is concrete and recurring; the math compounds
- **When to use:** B2B productivity tools, automation tools, anywhere manual work is the alternative
- **Vertical fit:** B2B SaaS, productivity tools, agencies (done-for-you positioning)

#### Lever F: Health / Vitality
- **The frame:** "Your body / mind / sleep / energy isn't what it could be."
- **Why it works:** Personal, physical, immediate
- **When to use:** Health, wellness, fitness, sleep, mental health
- **Compliance watch-out:** Health claims are FDA-regulated. "Supports" / "may help" not "cures" / "treats"
- **Vertical fit:** DTC health, supplements, fitness, wellness

#### Lever G: Status / Identity
- **The frame:** "Be the person who [outcome]."
- **Why it works:** Connects the offer to who they want to be, not just what they get
- **When to use:** Premium products, professional development, lifestyle brands
- **Vertical fit:** Info products, premium DTC, professional services, executive coaching

#### Lever H: Belonging / Community
- **The frame:** "Join [N] [people like you] who…"
- **Why it works:** Social proof + tribal identity in one beat
- **When to use:** Membership products, communities, niche-audience offers
- **Vertical fit:** Memberships, courses, niche DTC, B2B for tight communities

#### Lever I: Embarrassment / Stigma
- **The frame:** "You don't have to talk about [embarrassing thing] anymore."
- **Why it works:** Removes the shame barrier that prevents action
- **When to use:** Sensitive categories where prospects avoid even researching the problem
- **Vertical fit:** Hair loss, weight loss, sexual health, mental health, financial distress, addiction

---

### Category 3: Future-Focused Levers

These work by making the future more vivid — either threatening or aspirational.

#### Lever J: Inheritance / Family Burden
- **The frame:** "[Bad thing] could follow your family."
- **Why it works:** Activates protective instincts; especially powerful for older audiences
- **When to use:** Long-term obligations that could outlive the buyer; insurance; estate planning; legal preparation
- **Compliance watch-out:** Often requires qualified language ("may," "could") — never definitive
- **Vertical fit:** Life insurance, estate planning, long-term financial obligations, eldercare

#### Lever K: Opportunity Cost
- **The frame:** "Every [time period] you wait costs you [X]."
- **Why it works:** Activates loss aversion; makes inaction concrete
- **When to use:** Time-sensitive offers, compounding-benefit products
- **Vertical fit:** Investing, education, B2B SaaS (efficiency compounds), early-stage opportunities

#### Lever L: Aspirational Future
- **The frame:** "Imagine [vivid future state]."
- **Why it works:** Pulls visitors forward by making the dream outcome feel real
- **When to use:** Transformation products, lifestyle aspirations, premium experiences
- **Vertical fit:** Coaching, courses, fitness, financial freedom, travel, premium services

---

### Category 4: Trust & Authority Levers

These don't agitate pain — they build belief that the client is the right choice.

#### Lever M: Selectivity / Scarcity
- **The frame:** "We accept [N] of applicants. See if you qualify."
- **Why it works:** Cialdini scarcity + reframes from "buy from us" to "earn the right to be helped"
- **When to use:** High-trust-required offers, premium services, high-ticket
- **Watch-out:** Selectivity must be real. Fake gatekeeping is detectable and damaging.
- **Vertical fit:** Coaching, agencies, lead-gen for regulated services, premium B2B

#### Lever N: Founder Authority
- **The frame:** "Founded by someone who [credibility-establishing personal experience]."
- **Why it works:** Personal stake > corporate claim
- **When to use:** When the founder's experience directly maps to the customer's problem
- **Vertical fit:** Industry-insider-turned-advocate plays, founder-led agencies, expertise-driven services

#### Lever O: Established Track Record
- **The frame:** "Since [year]. [N] customers helped. [Big proof number]."
- **Why it works:** Volume + longevity = legitimacy
- **When to use:** When the client has real scale to claim
- **Vertical fit:** Universal — any client with multi-year track record

#### Lever P: Done-For-You Ease
- **The frame:** "We handle it end to end. You don't have to do anything."
- **Why it works:** Reduces effort & sacrifice (Hormozi); appeals to overwhelmed buyers
- **When to use:** When the client's offer is meaningfully more hands-off than alternatives
- **Vertical fit:** Agencies, services, managed software, premium DTC with concierge service

## How to Match Lever to Traffic Temperature

| Traffic | Lead With | Layer In |
|---|---|---|
| Cold (paid social, search) | Strongest external-villain or internal-pain lever | Selectivity, Authority |
| Warm (email, retargeting) | Hidden Cost or Trapped lever, with proof | Founder story, named outcomes |
| Hot (brand, direct) | Selectivity or Done-For-You | Process clarity, friction reducers |
| Older audience | Family-burden + financial pain | Health/age angle, founder story |
| Re-engagement (lost leads) | Acknowledge the gap + cost transparency | Address the prior friction reason directly |

---

## How to Match Lever to Awareness Level

| Schwartz Level | Best Lever Categories |
|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Status / Identity, Aspirational Future (educate first, sell later) |
| **Problem Aware** | Deception, Hidden Cost, Trapped, Time Waste — name the pain they feel |
| **Solution Aware** | Done-For-You, Selectivity, Founder Authority — differentiate within the category |
| **Product Aware** | Established Track Record, Selectivity, Risk Reversal — close the trust gap |
| **Most Aware** | Friction Reducers, Reciprocity (free bonus), Scarcity (act now) — remove the last barrier |

## How to Layer Levers

Single-lever pages convert worse than well-layered pages. Default structure:

1. **Hero:** ONE primary lever, hard. Don't dilute the headline by mixing levers.
2. **Pain section:** Reinforce the primary lever with specifics. Add 1 secondary lever if applicable.
3. **Proof / Trust:** Authority lever (Founder, Track Record, Selectivity)
4. **Process:** Done-For-You lever (effort reduction)
5. **Social proof:** Whatever lever the testimonials emphasize most
6. **Final CTA:** Friction reducers + (if applicable) Scarcity / Selectivity

Don't try to deploy 8 levers on one page. Pick 2–3 and run them deep.

## Validating Lever Choice for a New Client

If the client doesn't have lever-validation data (most don't), here are quick proxies:

| Validation Source | Strength | What It Tells You |
|---|---|---|
| Customer survey (n=200+) asking "what made you choose us" | Strongest | Direct lever validation |
| Sales call recordings (10+) | Strong | What objections come up; what emotional language customers use |
| Top-performing ad copy (last 90 days) | Moderate | What's been validated through ad performance |
| Top-converting landing page (last 90 days) | Moderate | Same as above, but slower-cycle data |
| Highest-rated reviews (50+) | Moderate | What emotional language customers use unprompted |
| Search query data (GSC, Ahrefs) | Weak-moderate | What pain points drive search demand |
| Competitor copy analysis | Weak | What others have validated; doesn't mean it's optimal |
| Vertical patterns from `industry-pattern-library.md` | Weak | Sensible defaults; not client-specific |

If the only available source is the vertical patterns, the lever is a hypothesis. State that explicitly. Recommend the client invest in primary research (surveys, customer interviews) before scaling spend behind a hypothesized lever.

## Lever-to-Copy Patterns

Once you've chosen a lever, here are proven copy patterns for deploying it:

### Deception Lever Patterns
- "The 3 [Biggest / Most Common] Lies [Industry] Tells [Audience]"
- "What [Industry] doesn't want you to know about [thing]"
- "You believed [thing]. Here's the truth."
- "They knew. They didn't tell you."

### Hidden Cost Lever Patterns
- "$X today. At [rate], that's $Y in [time period]."
- "The real cost of [thing] isn't what they told you."
- "Add it up: [calculation that reveals true cost]"
- "[N]% of [audience] don't realize they're paying for [hidden item]"

### Trapped Lever Patterns
- "Can't [sell / cancel / get out of] [thing]?"
- "[N] years in. Still paying."
- "[Thing] you can't escape — even though you've paid it off"

### Time Waste Lever Patterns
- "Stop spending [N hours] on [thing]"
- "Get back [time period] every [time period]"
- "[N] hours of [thing] → [smaller number] minutes"

### Selectivity Lever Patterns
- "We accept [N]% of applicants"
- "Currently [N] spots open"
- "Only available to [specific qualifier]"
- "See if you qualify"

### Founder Authority Lever Patterns
- "Our founder spent [N years] doing [thing]. Here's what they learned."
- "[Founder] used to [credibility-establishing previous role]. Then they saw [problem]. Now they help [audience]."
- "Industry insider. Founded [client] to [mission]."

---

## Anti-Patterns (Avoid These)

- **Mixing levers in the hero headline** — "Stop being lied to AND save time AND get your life back" — pick one
- **Generic pain without specificity** — "Tired of [thing]?" → no
- **Pain agitation with no path forward** — agitation without a clear next step is exhausting; always pivot to the offer
- **Aspirational framing for cold traffic** — cold visitors haven't bought into the dream yet; lead with pain or specificity
- **Selectivity that isn't real** — "Limited to 100 customers" when you'd take 10,000 is detectable and damaging
- **Founder stories that don't connect to the customer's problem** — if the founder's story doesn't map to why this offer exists, skip it
