# Website Page Framework — Main Site Pages

> **Purpose:** When auditing or building main website pages (homepage, About, Process, Reviews, FAQ, blog), the page type fundamentally changes the strategy. This framework defines how to evaluate and build each type. The landing page templates in `references/landing-page-templates.md` cover single-purpose pages. This document covers main website pages — the ones that live on the client's main marketing domain with full site navigation, serve mixed traffic, and must balance education with conversion.

---

## The Core Distinction

| | Single-Purpose Page | Main Website Page |
|---|---|---|
| **Examples** | Landing page, advertorial, quiz funnel, thank-you page | Homepage, Process, About Us, How It Works, Reviews, FAQ, Blog |
| **Navigation** | None. Zero escape routes. | Full site nav + footer links. Visitor can explore. |
| **Traffic mix** | One temperature (matched to the ad) | Mixed — cold from SEO, warm from email, hot from brand search |
| **Primary job** | Convert. One action, one CTA. | Educate AND convert. Multiple valid paths. |
| **Awareness range** | Narrow (matched to traffic source) | Wide — must serve Problem Aware through Most Aware |
| **CTA model** | Single repeated CTA | Primary CTA + secondary CTAs (learn more, read reviews, watch video) |
| **Content depth** | Lean. Just enough to move to action. | Deeper. Must answer questions visitors have before they're ready. |
| **Trust strategy** | Proof stack compressed into one scroll | Trust built across multiple sections and pages (internal linking matters) |
| **Conversion path** | Direct — form is on this page | May route TO a landing page or external form as the conversion action |
| **Testimonials** | 2–3 curated for the specific angle | More volume, more variety, woven throughout |
| **Compliance sensitivity** | High (usually broadcast-level) | High above fold; slightly more latitude in gated/deep content |
| **Grunt Test** | "What is this? What do I do?" = form | "What is this? What can I learn? Where do I go?" = explore or convert |

---

## How This Changes the Audit

### Scorecard Adjustments for Website Pages

The standard CRO scorecard (Clarity, Trust, Offer Strength, Emotional Resonance, Form Friction, Mobile) still applies, but the weights shift:

| Criterion | Landing Page Weight | Website Page Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Critical — 5-second Grunt Test | Critical — but "What do I do?" can mean "explore" | Visitors may be browsing, not ready to convert |
| **Trust** | Compressed proof stack | **Elevated** — this is where most trust is built | Website pages are where skeptics go to vet you |
| **Offer Strength** | Make-or-break | Less dominant — the "offer" is often "learn more" | Not every visitor is ready for the primary CTA |
| **Emotional Resonance** | Lead with one lever, hard | **Layer multiple levers** — different visitors, different pain | Mixed traffic means no single lever wins |
| **Form Friction** | Primary metric (form IS the page) | **De-emphasized** — form may not be on this page | CTA might route to a separate landing page or scheduler |
| **Navigation & IA** | N/A (no nav) | **New criterion** — is the site structure intuitive? | Bad nav = visitors can't find what they need |
| **Content Depth** | Minimal | **New criterion** — does it answer the questions visitors have? | Thin pages bounce, especially for SEO traffic |
| **Internal Linking** | N/A | **New criterion** — does it route visitors to the right next step? | A website page's job is often to move someone to the NEXT page |

### Add These to the Scorecard for Website Pages

- **Navigation & IA (1–10):** Is the site structure clear? Can visitors find what they need in 2 clicks?
- **Content Depth (1–10):** Does the page answer the questions its visitors are likely asking? Or does it leave gaps?
- **Internal Linking (1–10):** Does the page route visitors to logical next steps?
- **SEO Readiness (1–10):** Does the page have proper H1, meta, structured content for organic discovery?

### Drop or De-Emphasize

- **Form Friction** — score it only if the page HAS a form. Many website pages route to an external form. Evaluate the CTA and the path to conversion instead.
- **Selectivity Bar** — still effective but not mandatory on every website page. It belongs on the homepage and conversion-adjacent pages, not necessarily on About or FAQ.

## Website Page Templates

### Template W1: Homepage

**Traffic:** Mixed — cold (SEO, referral), warm (returning visitors), hot (brand search, direct)
**Awareness range:** All levels. The homepage must work for someone who's never heard of the client AND someone who's been in the nurture funnel for 3 months.
**Primary job:** Orient + route. "You're in the right place. Here's what we do and where to go next."
**Secondary job:** Convert warm/hot visitors who are ready now.

#### Section Order

**1. Hero Section**
- **Headline:** Authority + clarity hybrid for mixed traffic. Not a sharp pain-point hook (that's for landing pages). More like: "[Outcome] for [audience]," or "The [most-used / most-trusted / most-effective] [category] for [audience]."
- **Sub-headline:** 1 sentence that explains what the client does for the uninitiated. No jargon.
- **Primary CTA:** The main conversion goal from client context (for ready visitors)
- **Secondary CTA:** "See How It Works →" or "Read Customer Stories →" (for visitors who need more info first)
- **Trust stat line:** 3 stats from the client context's approved list
- Navigation stays. This is a website, not a landing page.

**2. "Who We Help" / Problem Recognition**
- Not aggressive pain agitation (save that for landing pages). More: validation.
- "If [common situation], you're not alone."
- Brief, empathetic. Lets the visitor self-select.
- This section speaks to ALL awareness levels without requiring one specific pain point.

**3. The Process / How It Works (Brief)**
- 3-step visual: Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3
- Slightly more detail than a landing page version — visitors came here to understand
- Emphasize what makes the process different
- Link: "Learn more about our process →" (routes to dedicated Process page if it exists)

**4. Trust & Proof Stack**
- Stats bar with the strongest 4–6 numbers from client context
- Founder story brief (3 sentences) with link to "Read [founder's] full story →"
- Press / media mentions if available
- Customer logo bar (recognized brands) if applicable
- Aggregate review widget (Trustpilot, G2, Google) if applicable

**5. Featured Outcomes / Differentiators**
- 2–3 specific things the client does that competitors don't
- Each backed by proof, not just claim
- Format options: bento grid of feature cards, side-by-side comparison vs. status quo, or named outcomes ("Cut [thing] by X%")

**6. Testimonials (3–5)**
- More variety than a landing page. Mix:
  - 1 short punchy (hero-adjacent)
  - 2 medium narrative (dedicated section)
  - 1 with named customer (credibility)
  - 1 that handles an objection (too expensive, takes too long, doesn't apply to me)
- Use `references/review-selector.md`. For homepage: maximize emotional-lever diversity (don't pick 3 testimonials all about the same thing).
- Star rating + total review count + link to full Reviews page

**7. FAQ Preview (3–5 questions)**
- The questions visitors ask before they're ready to convert
- Brief answers
- Link: "See all FAQs →"

**8. CTA Section (Bottom)**
- "Ready to [action]?"
- Primary CTA repeat
- Friction reducers from client context
- This is for visitors who scrolled the whole page and are now warm enough to act.

**9. Footer**
- Full navigation, contact info, disclaimers
- Trust badges
- Phone / email prominently displayed for hot visitors who want to reach out directly

#### Homepage Audit Checklist
- [ ] Does the hero work for BOTH first-time and returning visitors?
- [ ] Is there a clear path for visitors who are NOT ready to convert yet?
- [ ] Does the page establish trust within the first scroll?
- [ ] Are there multiple CTAs at different commitment levels (primary + secondary)?
- [ ] Does the page link out to deeper content (Process, Reviews, About)?
- [ ] Is contact info visible for hot traffic?
- [ ] Does it pass Grunt Test for mixed traffic: "What is this? Who is it for? What can I do?"

### Template W2: Process / How It Works

**Traffic:** Warm to hot — visitors who clicked "How It Works" from the homepage, an email, or organic search
**Awareness level:** Solution Aware to Product Aware — they know solutions exist, want to understand how this client specifically works
**Primary job:** Educate on the process. Build confidence that this is legitimate, structured, and professional.
**Secondary job:** Convert visitors who become confident enough to act.

#### Section Order

**1. Hero**
- Headline: "How [Client] [Does the Thing]" — direct, descriptive
- Sub-headline: 1 sentence with the strongest authority stat from client context
- No form in the hero. The visitor came to learn, not convert (yet). CTA: primary CTA as a button, not a form.

**2. Process Deep Dive (Expanded 3–5 Step)**
This is the Brunson "proprietary mechanism" expanded. Each step gets a full section:

For each step:
- **What happens in this step**
- **What the customer needs to do (minimal — emphasize ease)**
- **Timeline expectation**
- **What makes this step different from how others do it (if applicable)**

**3. What Makes This Different**
- Pull 3–5 differentiators from client context
- Frame each as a contrast: "Most [competitors] do X. We do Y because Z."
- This is where you stack Brunson's "named mechanism" against the alternative

**4. Common Questions About the Process**
- 4–6 of the most common process-related questions
- Honest, transparent answers
- This pre-handles the objections that would otherwise stop a conversion

**5. Testimonials (2–3)**
- Per `references/review-selector.md` — for process pages, prefer testimonials that handle process-confidence objections (takes too long, didn't think it would work, didn't know if it was legitimate)
- These visitors need process confidence, not emotional agitation
- Prefer specific-agent or specific-outcome attribution to reinforce the "real, professional process" promise

**6. CTA Section**
- "Ready to [action]?"
- Primary CTA → main conversion event
- Secondary: "Still have questions? [Talk to / Email] us at [contact]"

#### Process Page Audit Checklist
- [ ] Does each step answer: what happens, how long, what the customer does?
- [ ] Is the process framed as simple and low-effort for the customer?
- [ ] Are the differentiators specific, not generic?
- [ ] Are timeline expectations set honestly?
- [ ] Are testimonials matched to process confidence, not pain agitation?
- [ ] Is the next-step CTA clear?

### Template W3: About Us / Our Story

**Traffic:** Warm to hot — visitors vetting the client before committing
**Awareness level:** Product Aware — they know about the client, want to know if they TRUST the client
**Primary job:** Build deep trust. This is the "convince me you're real" page.
**Secondary job:** Soft convert through credibility.

#### Section Order

**1. Hero**
- Headline: A defining, trust-building statement. "Founded in [year]. [Big trust number from client context]."
- Sub-headline: 1 sentence on mission or origin

**2. Founder's Full Origin Story**
- This is the page where the founder story gets full space (3–5 paragraphs).
- Personal experience → turning point → mission → today
- Photo of founder. Human, approachable.
- Subject to client compliance constraints

**3. Company Milestones / Track Record**
- Timeline or milestone format with specific years and numbers
- These are proof of longevity and scale — critical for trust
- Examples: founding year, key awards, customer count milestones, revenue/impact milestones

**4. The Team**
- Communicate scale and professionalism (numbers, in-house vs. outsourced, geographic presence)
- Individual bios for key leaders if appropriate for the brand
- Team photos build trust faster than text

**5. Mission / Values**
- Keep it short and genuine. Avoid corporate mission-statement language.
- Tie back to brand voice from client context

**6. Testimonials (3–5)**
- Per `references/review-selector.md` — for About pages, prefer testimonials that handle skepticism: "I was skeptical, but..."
- These visitors are vetting. They need reviews from people who were ALSO skeptical and converted.
- Prefer medium narrative or long story format — this audience will read.

**7. CTA**
- Soft: "Ready to see if we can help?" → primary CTA
- Contact info prominently displayed

#### About Page Audit Checklist
- [ ] Does the founder story follow an emotionally engaging arc (not corporate filler)?
- [ ] Are compliance restrictions respected (per client context)?
- [ ] Is team scale communicated specifically (numbers, structure)?
- [ ] Are milestones specific (numbers, years), not vague ("many years of experience")?
- [ ] Do testimonials feature skepticism-overcome stories?
- [ ] Does the page feel human, not corporate?

### Template W4: Reviews / Testimonials Page

**Traffic:** Warm to hot — visitors specifically looking for social proof before converting
**Awareness level:** Product Aware to Most Aware — they're doing due diligence
**Primary job:** Overwhelm with proof. Volume matters here.
**Secondary job:** Convert the convinced.

#### Section Order

**1. Hero**
- Headline: Lead with the strongest aggregate proof number ("8,000+ Reviews. 6,000+ Five Stars.")
- Sub-headline: "See what our clients say about working with [client]."
- Aggregate rating display (Trustpilot, Google, G2, Capterra)

**2. Featured Reviews (3–5 premium testimonials)**
- Per `references/review-selector.md` — pull the strongest long-format reviews with specific outcomes, named customers, and emotional arcs
- Display as full-width cards with the excerpt prominently featured
- These set the credibility tone for the rest of the page

**3. Review Feed / Grid**
- Volume display — the more the better on this page
- Filter/sort options if technically feasible: by category, by star rating, by recency, by industry/segment
- Mix of review sources (Google, BBB, Trustpilot, G2, Capterra, etc.)
- This is where a tagged review database becomes a direct data source for a dynamic page

**4. Stats Bar (mid-page)**
- Top 3–4 trust numbers from client context
- Breaks up the review feed and reinforces scale

**5. CTA Section**
- "Join [number]+ [customers/clients/families] who've already taken the first step."
- Primary CTA → main conversion event
- This page's visitors are close to converting — the CTA should be confident, not cautious.

#### Reviews Page Audit Checklist
- [ ] Is the volume of reviews visually impressive (not just 3 quotes)?
- [ ] Are featured reviews diverse in emotional lever, audience segment, and format?
- [ ] Are review sources attributed (Google, BBB, etc.)?
- [ ] Are compliance redactions applied per client context (e.g., competitor names stripped)?
- [ ] Is there a clear CTA for visitors who've seen enough proof?

### Template W5: FAQ Page

**Traffic:** Mixed — SEO (long-tail questions), warm (email links), hot (pre-conversion)
**Awareness level:** Solution Aware to Product Aware
**Primary job:** Remove objections. Every unanswered question is a reason not to convert.
**Secondary job:** SEO value from question-based content.

#### Section Order

**1. Hero**
- Headline: "Frequently Asked Questions"
- Sub-headline: "Everything you need to know about [doing the thing] with [client]."

**2. FAQ Sections (Grouped by Theme)**
Common groupings:

**About the Process:**
- How does [the thing] work?
- How long does it take?
- What do I need to do during the process?
- What if [common concern]?

**About Cost & Value:**
- How much does it cost?
- Is this worth the money? (frame as: outcome value vs. cost)
- Are there any hidden fees?
- What if it doesn't work?

**About the Client (Company):**
- Is [client] legitimate?
- How long has [client] been in business?
- Who is [founder]?
- How many [customers/clients] have you helped?

**About the Industry / Alternatives:**
- Can I just do this myself?
- Why not use [generic alternative]?
- What about [common DIY approach]?

**After the Process:**
- What happens after [the thing] is done?
- What kind of ongoing support do I get?
- Can [common concern] happen later?

**3. CTA Section**
- "Still have questions? [Action]"
- Primary CTA
- Secondary: contact options

#### FAQ Page Audit Checklist
- [ ] Does every answer follow client compliance rules?
- [ ] Are answers concise (3–5 sentences max per question)?
- [ ] Are the highest-stakes questions answered clearly (cost, timeline, "is this real")?
- [ ] Is the page structured for SEO (H2 groups, H3 questions, FAQ schema-ready)?
- [ ] Does it link to deeper pages where appropriate?

## How to Determine Page Type During an Audit

When the user shares a URL, screenshot, or text and doesn't specify the page type, use this decision tree:

1. **Does it have site navigation (header menu, footer links)?**
   - No → Single-purpose page. Use `references/landing-page-templates.md`.
   - Yes → Website page. Continue below.

2. **Is it the root domain or the main entry point?**
   - Yes → **Homepage (W1)**

3. **Does it primarily explain how the service works?**
   - Yes → **Process / How It Works (W2)**

4. **Does it primarily tell the company's story, history, or team?**
   - Yes → **About Us (W3)**

5. **Does it primarily display customer reviews or testimonials?**
   - Yes → **Reviews Page (W4)**

6. **Does it primarily answer questions?**
   - Yes → **FAQ Page (W5)**

7. **Is it a blog post or content piece?**
   - Yes → Treat as content/SEO page. Apply W1 principles for the page shell, but evaluate content depth, SEO, and internal linking as primary criteria. The "CTA" is typically a sidebar or in-content offer.

8. **Pricing page?**
   - Treat as a hybrid. Apply the W1 trust-stacking rules and add pricing-specific patterns: anchored tiers, friction reducers per tier, money-back / trial language (compliance-permitting), comparison clarity.

9. **None of the above?**
   - Ask the user what the page's primary function is before auditing.

- **Conversion Path:** [Direct form on page | Routes to external form | Phone CTA | Multiple paths]

### 1. The Conversion Scorecard (Website Page Version)
- **Clarity (1–10):** [Miller] — Can mixed-traffic visitors orient in 5 seconds?
- **Trust (1–10):** [Kennedy] — Is authority established for skeptical visitors?
- **Content Depth (1–10):** — Does the page answer the questions its visitors have?
- **Emotional Resonance (1–10):** — Are multiple levers layered for mixed traffic?

## Key Principle: Website Pages Feed Landing Pages

The main website and single-purpose landing pages aren't competing — they're a system. The website educates, builds trust, and warms visitors up. Landing pages close them. The handoff between the two matters:

- Homepage → primary CTA → routes to landing page or scheduler
- Process → "Ready to [action]?" → routes to qualification quiz or main CTA
- Reviews → "Join [N] who've already…" → routes to main CTA
- FAQ → "Still have questions? [Action]" → routes to landing page or contact

When auditing a website page, always evaluate whether the **exit path to conversion** is clear and low-friction — even if the form isn't on the page itself.
